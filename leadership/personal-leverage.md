# Personal Leverage

## When to Use

- Evaluating career opportunities or job offers
- Deciding what skills to develop next
- Feeling trapped in time-for-money exchanges
- Planning a path to financial independence
- Choosing between immediate income and long-term equity
- Deciding whether to start a business, join early-stage, or stay employed

## Core Principle: Wealth vs. Money vs. Status

**Wealth** = Assets that earn while you sleep. Freedom.
**Money** = How we transfer wealth. A tool.
**Status** = Your rank in social hierarchy. A zero-sum game.

Seek wealth, not money or status. Status games are zero-sum and make you bitter. Wealth creation is positive-sum.

## The Three Types of Leverage

Leverage magnifies your outputs without magnifying your inputs. There are exactly three types:

| Type | Description | Permission Required | Scalability |
|------|-------------|--------------------|-------------|
| **Labor** | People working for you | Yes — must manage humans | Linear with team size |
| **Capital** | Money working for you | Yes — must raise or earn it | Scales with capital |
| **Products** | Code and media with zero marginal cost | No — permissionless | Infinite |

### Labor Leverage (Oldest Form)

Managing people. The traditional path to power.

**Upside:** Multiplies your output through others' effort.
**Downside:** Managing humans is hard. Requires leadership skill, creates obligations, scales linearly.

**When to use:** When you need diverse capabilities or when the work requires human judgment at scale.

### Capital Leverage (Industrial Age)

Money working on your behalf through investments, businesses, or financial instruments.

**Upside:** Money doesn't sleep, doesn't quit, doesn't need motivation.
**Downside:** Need capital first. Need judgment to deploy it well. Risk of loss.

**When to use:** When you have capital and judgment, or when you can raise capital based on track record.

### Product Leverage (Information Age)

Code, content, and media that cost nothing to replicate.

**Upside:** Write once, sell forever. No gatekeepers. Infinite scalability.
**Downside:** Winner-take-most dynamics. Requires specific knowledge to create something valuable.

**This is the leverage to prioritize.** It's permissionless — you don't need anyone's permission to write code, publish content, or build a product. The marginal cost of the next user/reader/customer approaches zero.

## Specific Knowledge

The most important concept for personal leverage. Specific knowledge is knowledge that cannot be trained for. If society can train you for it, it can replace you.

**Characteristics:**
- Feels like play to you, looks like work to others
- Found by pursuing genuine curiosity, not following career tracks
- Often at the intersection of fields
- Built through apprenticeship and experience, not school
- Cannot be outsourced or automated

**Examples:**
- Deep technical + domain expertise combination
- Unique taste or judgment in a field
- Rare skill combinations (coding + design + sales)
- Pattern recognition from years of specific experience

**The question:** "What do I know or do that feels easy to me but hard to others?"

## The Accountability Equation

Take business risks under your own name. Society rewards those who take accountability.

**Accountability = leverage:**
- Builds reputation (portable across opportunities)
- Attracts opportunities you can't find by looking
- Compounds over time
- Requires courage (downside is visible)

**Contrast:** Hiding in committees, anonymous work, blaming others — no upside capture. The people who get the biggest rewards are those willing to be publicly wrong.

## The Equity Imperative

You will not get rich renting out your time. You must own equity — a piece of a business — to gain financial freedom.

**Time-for-money traps:**
- Salary caps your upside
- You're replaceable
- No compound growth
- Trading a finite resource (time) for money

**Equity paths:**
1. Start a business
2. Join early-stage companies for equity
3. Create intellectual property
4. Build products with zero marginal cost

**The test for any opportunity:** Does this build my equity position, or just pay me for my time?

## Compound Growth

Play long-term games with long-term people.

**Compounding applies to:**
- **Relationships** — Trust builds over time and opens doors
- **Knowledge** — Foundations enable advanced learning
- **Reputation** — Track record compounds like interest
- **Wealth** — Returns compound exponentially

**Implication:** Optimize for the long term. Don't burn relationships for short-term gain. Don't take shortcuts that sacrifice reputation. All returns in life come from compound interest.

## The Hourly Rate Heuristic

Set an aspirational hourly rate for yourself. Outsource or eliminate anything below it.

**If your target is $500/hr:**
- Don't mow your lawn ($50/hr task)
- Don't spend 3 hours optimizing a $100 savings
- Don't take meetings with unclear value
- Do invest time in learning that raises your rate

**Why it works:** Forces prioritization. Your time is your ultimate finite resource.

**Caution:** Don't use this to avoid learning new skills that will raise your rate long-term.

## Judgment: The Meta-Skill

Judgment — knowing the long-term consequences of your actions — is the most valuable skill. People pay for decisions, not labor.

**Judgment comes from:**
- Experience (especially failures)
- Learning foundational concepts (not news)
- Mental models from multiple disciplines
- Clear thinking through meditation, reading, solitude

**Enemies of clear thinking:**
- Ego (protecting self-image)
- Emotions (short-term reactivity)
- Social pressure (wanting approval)
- Complexity (not understanding fundamentals)

**Aids to clear thinking:**
- First principles (strip to basics)
- Inversion (what would guarantee failure?)
- Long time horizons (how does this look in 10 years?)
- Solitude (your own thoughts, not others')

## Decision Framework for Opportunities

### The Leverage Audit

For any career move or opportunity, score these dimensions:

| Dimension | Question | Score (1-5) |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Specific knowledge** | Does this build knowledge that can't be trained for? | |
| **Equity** | Do I own a piece of the upside, or just trade time? | |
| **Accountability** | Am I building reputation under my own name? | |
| **Leverage type** | Am I gaining labor, capital, or product leverage? | |
| **Compounding** | Will this compound over 5-10 years? | |
| **Freedom** | Does this increase or decrease my optionality? | |
| **Long-term people** | Am I playing with people I'll work with for decades? | |
| **Authenticity** | Would I do this if I couldn't tell anyone? | |

### Decision Heuristics

**Easy choices, hard life. Hard choices, easy life.** When evenly split, take the harder path — your brain overweights short-term difficulty.

**If you can't decide, the answer is no.** Modern life has too many options. Saying no to most things gives you time for what matters.

**Evaluate the upside/downside asymmetry.** Seek convex opportunities: limited downside, uncapped upside. Avoid concave ones: limited upside, uncapped downside.

## Application Checklist

- [ ] Identify your specific knowledge — what feels like play to you?
- [ ] Audit current role: am I building equity or renting time?
- [ ] Identify which leverage type I'm primarily using (labor/capital/products)
- [ ] Am I taking accountability under my own name?
- [ ] Set an aspirational hourly rate and eliminate tasks below it
- [ ] Evaluate current relationships: am I playing long-term games with long-term people?
- [ ] Check: is my current path compounding, or linear?
- [ ] What would I need to change to shift from labor/capital leverage to product leverage?

## Anti-Patterns

**The Golden Handcuffs** — High salary with no equity, no specific knowledge building, no reputation under your name. Comfortable but compounding nothing.

**The Status Game** — Optimizing for title, prestige, or social ranking instead of wealth, freedom, and specific knowledge. Status is zero-sum and makes you bitter.

**The Credentials Treadmill** — Collecting degrees and certifications. Specific knowledge can't be certified. If they can teach it in a classroom, it's not specific knowledge.

**Short-Term Optimization** — Taking the highest-paying option without considering compounding effects. 5 years of $200k salary with no equity < 3 years of $120k + meaningful equity.

**Renting Time to the Highest Bidder** — Freelancing or consulting without building assets, reputation, or equity. Your income stops the moment you stop working.

**The "Someday" Fallacy** — "I'll start building equity someday, after I save enough." You start building leverage now, or you don't. There's no someday.
