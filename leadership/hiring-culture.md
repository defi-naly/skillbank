# Hiring and Culture

## When to Use

- Hiring for a role and evaluating candidates
- Deciding whether to fire an underperformer or cultural misfit
- Designing or evolving company culture
- Running one-on-ones and giving feedback
- Dealing with high-performing but toxic individuals
- Scaling a team from 5 to 50+ without losing what made it work

## Core Principle: Hire for Strength, Not Lack of Weakness

Don't hire safe candidates who lack obvious flaws. Hire people with spiky strengths, even if they have weaknesses.

**Mediocrity**: Someone with no major weaknesses but no major strengths.
**Excellence**: Someone exceptional at what matters, even if imperfect elsewhere.

The question isn't "Is this person smart?" but "Do they know how to do the specific thing I need?"

## Hiring Framework

### What to Evaluate

| Factor | Question | How to Assess |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| **Functional knowledge** | Do they actually know how to do this job? | Work samples, scenario questions |
| **Spiky strength** | Where are they clearly exceptional? | References, past work, deep-dive questions |
| **Learning velocity** | How fast do they acquire new skills? | Past examples of rapid adaptation |
| **Character skills** | How do they handle feedback, failure, ambiguity? | Behavioral questions, back-channel references |
| **Trajectory** | Are they improving or coasting? | Career progression, recent learning |
| **Cultural fit** | Will they thrive in YOUR specific culture? | Values discussion, trial period |

### Reference Checks That Matter

Back-channel references matter more than provided references.

**Questions that reveal:**
- "Would you hire this person again? For what role?"
- "What would I need to know to manage them well?"
- "Where did they struggle?"
- "How did they handle [difficult situation relevant to your role]?"

### Assessing Potential, Not Just Current Ability

Don't just evaluate where candidates are — assess how far and fast they'll travel.

**Character skills that predict growth:**
- **Proactive absorption** — Do they seek feedback, or wait for it?
- **Determination** — How have they persisted through difficulty?
- **Deliberate play** — Do they find joy in learning and practicing?
- **Intellectual humility** — Can they say "I was wrong" and mean it?

**The question:** Given equal starting points, who would travel further in 18 months?

## Firing

### When to Fire

- The person consistently misses clear expectations
- You've given direct feedback and nothing changed
- The role has outgrown the person and they can't grow into it
- Cultural mismatch is affecting the team
- You find yourself regularly wondering whether to fire them (you already know)

### How to Fire

1. **Don't drag it out.** Once you know, act within days.
2. **Be direct.** "This isn't working. Today is your last day."
3. **Be generous.** Good severance, references, job search support.
4. **Take responsibility.** "I made a bad hire" or "The role changed."
5. **Don't make it personal.** This is about fit, not worth.

### The Rule of Twos

If two people independently tell you someone needs to go, they probably do. If you've thought about firing someone twice, you should.

## Culture

### Culture Is Behavior, Not Values on a Wall

Culture is what people do when you're not there. It's defined by:

- **Who you hire** (and fire) first
- **What behaviors you tolerate**
- **What gets rewarded and promoted**
- **How decisions get made**
- **What you say no to**

### Design Virtues, Not Values

Values are abstract. Virtues are concrete behaviors.

| Value (Vague) | Virtue (Specific) |
|--------------|-------------------|
| "We value honesty" | "Bad news travels fast" |
| "We value collaboration" | "Disagree, then commit" |
| "We value excellence" | "Ship on the date, or renegotiate the date — never silently slip" |
| "We value innovation" | "Every quarter, kill one thing that's working to make room for something that might work better" |

### Culture Debt

Just like technical debt, you accrue culture debt by tolerating behavior that violates your standards. It compounds.

**Examples of culture debt:**
- Not addressing a brilliant jerk's behavior
- Letting mediocre work ship because the person "is trying"
- Skipping one-on-ones because you're "too busy"
- Hiring for speed instead of fit during growth

**Fix violations immediately.** One exception becomes the new rule.

## Managing

### One-on-Ones

The most important meeting you have. It's their meeting, not yours.

**Structure:**
1. "What's on your mind?" — They set the agenda
2. "What's the biggest impediment to your work?"
3. "What feedback do you have for me?"

**Rules:**
- Weekly for direct reports. Never skip.
- Listen more than you talk
- Follow up on previous items
- Use it to detect problems early

### Giving Feedback

The most important management skill. Everything else follows from your ability to tell people directly what's working and what isn't.

**The framework:**
- **Be direct** — The feedback sandwich is a lie. Burying criticism between compliments dilutes the message.
- **Be specific** — Not "do better" but "in yesterday's presentation, the data on slide 3 was wrong. Here's what I need you to do differently."
- **Be timely** — Feedback 6 months later is useless. Feedback today is actionable.
- **Don't soften so much the message is lost** — Clarity is kindness.

**But also (from Grant's research):**
- Seek feedback yourself first — model vulnerability
- Ask "What's one thing I could do better?" regularly
- Create psychological safety so people will tell you truth
- Treat feedback as data, not judgment

### Managing Brilliant Jerks

Exceptional performers who are toxic to culture present a false dilemma.

**The truth**: Tolerating jerks tells everyone that performance matters more than values. Culture erodes. Good people leave. The remaining team performs worse because psychological safety is destroyed.

**The answer**: Coach clearly ("This specific behavior needs to change by this date"), then fire if no change. No one is irreplaceable.

## Building Culture at Scale

### The First 10 Hires

These people define your culture permanently. Hire them as if the entire company's identity depends on it — because it does.

**Priorities for early hires:**
- Alignment with founding beliefs and values
- Generalist capability (early teams need people who can do many things)
- Comfort with ambiguity and chaos
- High ownership — people who act like founders, not employees

### Scaling from 10 to 100

The moment where most cultures break. What worked informally must become intentional.

**What to systematize:**
- Onboarding that transmits culture (not just process)
- Feedback systems that scale beyond founder-to-everyone
- Decision-making frameworks that don't require founder approval
- Promotion criteria that reinforce cultural virtues

**What to protect:**
- The specific behaviors that made early culture work
- Small team dynamics within larger organization
- Direct access to leadership
- Speed of decision-making

### The Leader's Role in Culture

You don't get to opt out of culture work once the team grows. Culture is your product.

**Your ongoing responsibilities:**
- Model the behavior you expect
- Address violations visibly and quickly
- Hire and fire based on cultural fit, not just performance
- Tell stories that reinforce what matters
- Make decisions consistent with stated virtues

## Application Checklist

- [ ] Define the specific spiky strength needed for this hire
- [ ] Conduct back-channel reference checks, not just provided ones
- [ ] Assess character skills (absorption, determination, humility) alongside functional skills
- [ ] Write cultural virtues as specific behaviors, not abstract values
- [ ] Run weekly one-on-ones where the report sets the agenda
- [ ] Address culture violations within 48 hours
- [ ] Give feedback that is direct, specific, and timely
- [ ] If debating whether to fire someone — you already know the answer

## Anti-Patterns

**The Safe Hire** — Choosing candidates with no red flags over candidates with exceptional strengths. You get mediocrity.

**Culture Fit as Conformity** — "Culture fit" used to mean "people like us." Real culture fit is about shared values and behaviors, not demographics or personality type.

**Skipping the Awkward Conversation** — Avoiding direct feedback because it's uncomfortable. The kindest thing is clarity. Leaving someone to fail slowly is crueler than telling them directly.

**The Scaling Identity Crisis** — Trying to preserve the exact culture of 10 people when you're 200. Culture evolves. Preserve the principles, adapt the practices.

**Hiring for Now, Not Trajectory** — Someone who's perfect today but can't grow is a future firing. Assess learning velocity, not just current skill.
